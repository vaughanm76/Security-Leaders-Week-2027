Why the week exists

The regulatory baseline is necessary. It is not sufficient. Requirements are written against threats already understood and vulnerabilities already demonstrated, so by construction they lag the threat rather than anticipate it. An operator that meets every requirement in full may still be exposed to what has not yet been written down. Compliance establishes a floor, not a defence.

Security Leaders Week works above that floor, in the space where the regulator cannot mandate and the airline cannot act alone. That space is only reachable through the airline–regulator relationship: partnership provides the framework, collaboration follows, trust grows out of collaboration that proves reliable, and trust is what lets an operator name a vulnerability it has not yet solved and a regulator hear it as intelligence rather than a finding.

Sydney 2025 set the format. Chicago 2026 consolidated it. London carries a defined agenda, named problem owners, and outcomes that land in the Annual Security Report and the ICAO High Level Security Conference cycle. A working session with a record, not a convening.

Tuesday morning and afternoon Defining the problems

Naming what is unresolved: regulatory divergence and More Stringent Measures, one-stop security, the evolving threat, cyber, UAS, inconsistent oversight and the supply chain. Honest problem definition is the harder half of the work and the part most often skipped.

Wednesday morning Working the solutions

What is available now: risk-based rather than rule-based oversight, security management systems as the shared language of airline risk, verifiable digital trust between operators and authorities, and mutual recognition where standards genuinely align.

Wednesday afternoon Setting the stretch targets

Where practice should be in three to five years, stated deliberately beyond current capability. Stretch targets are what a room of leaders can commit to that a regulatory process cannot yet require.

House rules

Chatham House Rule. Information shared may be used. The identity and affiliation of the speaker, and of any other participant, may not be revealed. This applies to social media posts as much as to any subsequent reporting or internal circulation.

Recording. No audio or video recording of sessions. Slides are released after the event only with the presenter's consent.

Social media. Delegates and both organisations may post about the event's themes and their own participation. No naming, quoting or identifying another participant without their agreement, and no photography that captures other delegates without their consent.

Delegate list. Not published or circulated outside IATA, the host and confirmed delegates. Name and organisation only in this document.

Media. No journalists are invited and none will attend in a reporting capacity. The host's editorial and analyst functions are separate from its hosting role; attendance confers no reporting access. Any press release after the event will be agreed by IATA and the host, and will not name delegates or attribute remarks.

Competition law. The Competition Law Guidelines for IATA Industry Meetings apply and are stated at the opening.

Invitation and visa letters, dietary or access requirements, and any unplanned media approach during the event: aviationsecurity@iata.org. The IATA communications lead is named in the delegate pack.
